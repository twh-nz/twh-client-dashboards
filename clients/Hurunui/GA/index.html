Facilitator script – read aloud or paraphrased at the interview open · ~90 seconds

The picture coming through is strong in real places. Your 2018 Constitution and Charter give the marae a thorough legal foundation. The board is actively building its policies – the Conflict of Interest Policy and Code of Conduct are recent, well made and in use. Your 2024/25 Strategic Plan tells one clear story about te reo. The picture so far

The strength to build on, and the shape of the work ▾

The strength to build on. Hurunui o Rangi is a small, volunteer-run marae. Six trustees do both governance and operations, and the hui runs on portfolios – finances, compliance, papakāinga, buildings, events, te reo. The 2018 Constitution and Charter are genuinely strong. The 2024–25 policy run shows a board building its governance one well-made document at a time. Tikanga is held in the hui. The kaupapa record is real – Pā Wars, Kura Reo, the papakāinga paid off. Connection to whānau and community is the highest-rated area in the whole survey, and the only one both halves of the board agree is strong. Five of six trustees independently name the new marae build as the top priority.

The shape of the work. Both lenses show the same picture. The foundations and the kaupapa fire are real. What needs building is the working level underneath them: who may decide and spend what, the two policies the law expects (Health & Safety and risk), a strategy the board reviews rather than files away, and meeting habits that turn decisions into tracked actions. Underneath all of it sits what the survey says loudest – the board needs an agreed way to work through disagreement, and enough trustees in the room for decisions to be made at all.

Gaps here are read as opportunities, not deficiencies. Documents a small marae has not yet produced or shared – a consolidated Governance Manual, an Annual Budget, a full Board Pack – are noted as not-yet-built or not-yet-seen, never marked as poor.

1Agree a working covenant – a tikanga-grounded way through disagreementTwo-source certain▶
📄 Document signal

The Deed has no tikanga-based path through disagreement – its dispute clause only sends interpretation questions back to the trustees. The 2025 Code of Conduct carries named values and a breach process for conduct; disagreement more broadly has no agreed way through.

📊 Survey signal
  • An "effective process for conflict resolution when things get heated or tense" is ticked as missing by five of six trustees.
  • Managing disagreement & tension – all six 3.00 · lower four 2.25.
  • The Chair: "a lot of individualism, not working together, personality-driven decisions, little respect for each other." Kim asks for ways "to uplift one another instead of being obstructive."
🗣 Interviews will test

What that individualism looks like in a hui (Justina). What it looks like around the table, at the level of how the board works (Kim). What Mei has seen when the board disagrees – and why Josephine reads the same room at 5.

A practical starting point

A facilitated session – separate from a business hui – agreeing two or three written commitments: how disagreement is voiced, how decisions are owned once made, and what happens when the line is crossed. Reviewed at each hui.
