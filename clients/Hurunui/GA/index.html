Prep · Interim

This is the prep dashboard – a two-of-three-lens view, built for the facilitator, not the board. It draws together the document reviews and the trustee survey to orient the facilitator before the kōrero. The interview lens is still to come; nothing here is a settled conclusion. The next products are the draft dashboard (after interviews) and the client dashboard (final, board-facing). Names are retained because this is an internal working view.

🎙 Facilitator script – read aloud at interview open ~90 seconds

"Thank you for the eight documents and the six survey responses you've shared with us – one hundred and sixty-two scored answers plus your written kōrero. The picture coming through is strong in real places: your 2018 Constitution and Charter give the marae a thorough legal foundation; the board is actively building its policy layer – the Conflict of Interest policy and Code of Conduct are recent, well made and in use; and your connection to whānau and community is the highest-rated area in the survey, with the new marae build and te reo the shared kaupapa across the board. The opportunities to grow into are the machinery underneath that fire – making the money fully visible to every trustee, agreeing how the board works through disagreement, and the meeting basics that keep decisions moving: quorum, papers, actions. What we're hoping to learn from you today is what the data alone can't tell us – why the board reads itself so differently from seat to seat, what disagreement looks like in the room, and what information reaches you before a hui."

The strength to build on. Hurunui o Rangi is a small, volunteer-run marae where six trustees do both governance and operations – the hui runs on portfolios (finances, compliance, papakāinga, buildings, events, te reo). Its 2018 constitutional architecture is genuinely strong, and the 2024–25 policy run shows a board actively building its governance layer one well-made document at a time. Tikanga is held in the hui, the kaupapa record is real – Pā Wars, Kura Reo, the papakāinga paid off – and connection to whānau and community is the highest-rated thing in the whole survey: the one area both halves of the board agree is strong. Five of six trustees independently name the new marae build as the top priority.

The shape of the work. Across both lenses, the same picture appears: the foundations and the kaupapa fire are real; what is thin is the operating layer that turns intention into tracked, lawful action – who may decide and spend what, the two legal-duty policies, a held strategy rhythm, decision-and-action discipline in the hui – and, underneath all of it, what the survey says loudest: an agreed way for six trustees to work through disagreement, and enough of them in the room for decisions to be made at all.

Gaps here are read as opportunities, not deficiencies. Documents a small marae has not yet produced or shared (a consolidated governance manual, a budget document, a full board pack) are noted as not-yet-built or not-yet-seen – never marked as poor.

Snapshot

Strengths to hold, areas to build, and the questions only the interviews can answer.

✓ Strengths to hold

  • A thorough, well-structured 2018 legal foundation – nineteen Deed items clearly in place.
  • An active, recent policy build – Conflict of Interest 2024 (with a working register), Code of Conduct 2025 – one well-made document at a time.
  • Tikanga held in the hui (karakia bookends, te reo throughout) and continuity strong – minutes, matters arising, next hui scheduled.
  • Whānau and community connection is the highest-rated area (pathways 4.17 / 5) – and the only one both halves of the board agree on.
  • A shared kaupapa: five of six trustees independently name the new marae build, with te reo alongside.

↗ Areas to build

  • The money layer – who may decide and spend what, and reporting every trustee can see (a delegation policy is already in motion).
  • An agreed, tikanga-grounded way through disagreement – the survey's keystone.
  • Quorum and the people pipeline – hui already postponed, decisions waiting.
  • The two legal-duty policies (Health & Safety, risk) and the meeting decision-and-action habits.
  • A held strategy rhythm so the planning wānanga's output doesn't go quiet.

🗣 To surface in interviews

  • Why this board reads itself two ways – four trustees average 2.0–3.2, two sit above 4.2 – and it is not the usual newcomer pattern: the one new trustee is the lowest scorer.
  • What disagreement looks like in the room, and whether attendance decay is its symptom.
  • The delegation-and-mana tension around the financial policy in motion.
  • What financial reporting actually reaches trustees – a blind spot no lens has seen.
  • Whether the protections four trustees can't see exist on paper, or don't exist at all.
