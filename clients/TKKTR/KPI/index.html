Grow Revenue

“Grow revenue by 10% in the year to 30 June 2026.”

IWhat this KPI meansWhat the KPI asks for, and why your board set it▾
It means
Total revenue for the year is at least 10% higher than in the baseline year. The audited financial statements confirm the result.
It does not mean
Growth of any kind. One-off contract income can meet the 10% and still leave the organisation no better funded the next year. That is why the evidence map splits recurring income from one-off income.
Why your board set it
1A and 1B are a pair: 1A tracks revenue and 1B tracks the margin. Together they show how the board is looking after the organisation's financial resources. Every service the organisation runs depends on its revenue.
Why it is a stretch
The 2025–27 contract envelope is about $1.37 million, and it stays broadly flat from year to year. So most of the 10% would need to come from new revenue, which is what the pipeline of funding bids tracks.
What it protects against
Finding out about a funding shortfall only when the audit arrives. It also protects against a growth story that rests on forecasts alone.
Link to the strategy
Financial sustainability. The Strategic Plan's priority targets can be added here once the board confirms them.

IIILeading indicators and tripwiresHow your board would see early if this KPI is going off track▾

A revenue target only protects the board if the board can see a shortfall while there is still time to act.

Leading indicator
The forecast of funding bids (RFPs) and their total value. The board chose this at the June 2026 workshop. If the pipeline of bids shrinks, the board would see a shortfall coming well before the audited result.
Read together
The CEO's reports would show three numbers side by side: (i) the total value of live bids, (ii) how many bids become contracts, and (iii) revenue compared with the same period last year. A forecast on its own can mislead, because a bid only becomes revenue once it is won.
Lagging indicator
The audited financial statements. They confirm the result independently, but only after the year is over.

Proposed tripwires, for your board to confirm

TripwireWhen it is crossedWhat would happen
Too few bids in the pipelineAt the end of any quarter, the value of live bids is less than three times the revenue still needed to reach 10%.The Audit, Finance and Risk Committee would review the pipeline with the CEO and report to the next board hui.
Bids are not becoming contractsTwo quarters in a row pass with no bid won and no other new revenue.The board would ask the CEO for a revised revenue plan, e.g. more bids, different funders or other income.
Revenue is behind at the half-yearRevenue is tracking below about 5% growth for the year, and no seasonal catch-up has been minuted.The board would agree an action to address the shortfall, and minute it.

VIChanging this KPIHow the Change Protocol in the KPI Policy applies to this KPI▾

This KPI follows the Change Protocol in the KPI Policy. These notes apply to it.

  • Because this is a financial KPI, the Audit, Finance and Risk Committee would usually be the committee that recommends a change to the board.
  • The parts most likely to need changing are the leading indicator and its tripwire levels. The 10% target itself is less likely to change.
  • One proposal is already open. In November 2025 the CEO proposed a rolling 12-month target, and the board has not yet decided on it. A decision either way would go in the decision register.

IWhat this KPI meansWhat the KPI asks for, and why your board set it▾
It means
The net profit margin stays at or above 5% all the way to 30 June 2026. The audited financial statements confirm the result.
It does not mean
A 5% margin reached by putting off needed spending or reducing service quality. A short note on any spending put off would guard against this (see section II).
Why your board set it
1B is the second half of the financial pair with 1A. Revenue can grow while costs grow faster. The margin shows whether the organisation is living within its means.
What it protects against
Finding out about a squeeze on the margin only at the end of the year. It also protects against a year that looks profitable only because spending was put off.

IIILeading indicators and tripwiresHow your board would see early if this KPI is going off track▾

A problem with the margin can build for months without anyone noticing. Costs rise a little, income drops a little, and the board often hears about it first in the audit.

Leading indicator
None chosen yet. The bids forecast used for 1A shows where revenue is heading, but not where costs are heading. Two options would show the margin early: the monthly management accounts, or the cost-to-income ratio (the share of income spent on costs) compared with budget.
Lagging indicator
The audited financial statements. They confirm the result independently, after the year is over.

Proposed tripwires, for your board to confirm

TripwireWhen it is crossedWhat would happen
Margin below 5%The monthly management accounts show a net margin below 5% for two months in a row.The CEO would explain the causes and bring a plan for costs and income to the Audit, Finance and Risk Committee.
Costs running ahead of budgetAt the end of any quarter, the cost-to-income ratio is above budget.The Audit, Finance and Risk Committee would review it with the CEO and report to the next board hui.
Margin held by putting off spendingThe 5% is met while planned investment, maintenance or service quality is put off.The CEO would report the spending put off as a one-line note beside the margin, so the board sees both.

IWhat this KPI meansWhat the KPI asks for, and why your board set it▾
It means
By September 2026, there are two new strategic partnerships, and each one is already delivering a joint initiative, project or service.
Who counts
Any organisation that the board confirms as a strategic partner. An MOU is not required.
It does not mean
Counting signed MOUs. A partnership counts only once it delivers something together with the organisation.
Why your board set it
Partnerships bring the organisation reach, shared delivery and funding. 6A tracks whether new partnerships are being formed. 6B tracks whether partnerships keep working, and 6C tracks whether whaiora are better off.
What it protects against
Signed agreements that do not lead to any shared work.

IIILeading indicators and tripwiresHow your board would see early if this KPI is going off track▾

The partner survey happens once a year. Without earlier signs, the board's first warning of a struggling partnership would be the low rating itself, a year too late to help.

Leading indicators
How regularly catch-ups happen, joint initiatives delivered, and referrals made. If these slow down, the board would see that a rating might slip before the annual survey.
Read together
The board would look at the work and its effect together. Catch-ups can be frequent while a partner still rates the relationship poorly. Either sign on its own can mislead.
Lagging indicators
The annual effectiveness rating (the share of partnerships rated effective or highly effective) and the survey score. Both come from the independent survey.
