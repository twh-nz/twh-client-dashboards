🎤 Facilitator script — read aloud at interview open (~90 seconds)

"Thank you for the 3 documents — Trust Charter, agendas and minutes, the policy library — and the 5 survey responses you've shared. We want to start by naming what's strong. Your Charter is culturally grounded, most core elements clearly in place. The kaupapa heart is real — 'meetings focus on the needs and priorities of our kaupapa' is your highest-scored survey item, and whānau-and-community connection is your strongest area overall. The hui is alive too — decisions land, the Chair tables real choices, and the Pou table is a stronger role-clarity foundation than most marae start with."

"What the data is asking for is the governance machinery underneath the manaaki — strategy made visible, a rhythm trustees can rely on, the operational fires (tax debt, returns, treasurer gap, asset safety) sorted, and a tikanga-grounded path through disagreement. What we're hoping to learn today is what those gaps look like from inside the room, where the load could be more fairly carried, and what would help most over the next year."

The strength to build on. Te Ore Ore is a working marae with a strong founding Charter, a kaupapa heart that is still beating, and a Chair-led hui where real decisions are tabled and followed through. The kaupapa-focus item ("meetings focus on the needs and priorities of our kaupapa") is the highest-scored item in the whole survey, and whānau-and-community connection is the strongest dimension. The Pou (portfolio) document already gives the board more role-clarity scaffolding than most marae start with.

The shape of the work. Across both lenses, the same picture comes through: the kaupapa is loved and the discussions are happening — what is thin is the governance machinery underneath, the layer that lets trustees see strategy, finance, risk and accountability on paper rather than holding it all in the Chair's head. The picture is also unusually consistent — all five survey respondents converge low (personal means 1.63–2.23), with 19 of 27 scored items below the 2.5 red-flag line. When five people see the same picture, the gaps are real, not contested.

Gaps here are read as opportunities, not deficiencies. Some live operational fires (tax debt, overdue GST/charities returns, treasurer gap, end-of-life buildings) need attention now and are surfaced as risks; the structural fixes are paced for a board with no paid staff.

Snapshot

Strengths to hold, areas to build, and the questions only the interviews can answer.

✓ Strengths to hold

  • An exceptionally strong founding Charter — 17 core elements clearly in place, te ao Māori framing, collective decision-making.
  • The kaupapa heart is intact — "meetings focus on the needs and priorities of our kaupapa" is the highest-scored item in the survey (3.40 / 5).
  • Whānau and community connection is the strongest dimension (Mahau 2.48) — hosting kaupapa 3.00, pathways for whānau 2.80.
  • A working board pack in motion — Chair tables a wide range of matters, follows decisions through, brings real choices into the hui.
  • The Pou (portfolio) document is a stronger role-clarity foundation than most marae start with; the Administration Pou row already functions as a complete Board Secretary description.

↗ Areas to build

  • Strategy is invisible — no agreed priorities, no reporting structure to see progress (the lowest cluster in the survey).
  • The governance machinery is unwritten — risks, financials, options, resolutions are held by the Chair rather than circulated as papers before each hui.
  • Real operational fires are burning right now — tax debt, overdue GST and charities returns, treasurer resigned, kauta and wharepaku end-of-life.
  • The governance / operations line is unwritten and the Chair is carrying the load — "delegation falls on deaf ears", "Chair in charge, board not aware of actions taken or not taken".
  • Simple controls (conflicts of interest, financial delegations, trustee protections, a tikanga way through disagreement) are not written down anywhere.

🗣 To surface in interviews

  • The 5-of-~9 trustee response rate — what is happening with attendance, quorum, and the wider board (and who hasn't engaged with the Tirohanga).
  • The Chair-and-daughter dynamic (Piriniha & Belinda) — where the load lands and where the frustration sits.
  • What "a fire dumpster waiting to explode" and "delegation falls on deaf ears" look like in the room — and what a tikanga-grounded path through them would look like.
  • The relationship between the Marae Reservation Trust and the separate Charitable Trust — only Dany can clarify this.
  • Whether the strong sub-board strategic content is being captured anywhere, or only in the Chair's head.
